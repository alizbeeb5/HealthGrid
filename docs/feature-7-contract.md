# Feature 7 — Investment package planning

Status: implementation plan; the investments module and screen are placeholders.
Feature 6 already provides saved resource-transfer proposals. This milestone
builds on those contracts without changing their results.

## Purpose and bounded first release

Answer “What should we fund to close the remaining capacity gap?” Compare doing
nothing further with a budget-constrained package of supplied investment projects.
Show selected projects, dependencies, full modeled cost, time to availability,
capacity before/after and the unmet demand that remains.

First release: one service (`emergency-care`), the frozen post-allocation scenario,
one evaluation date and a small manually reviewed project catalog. Support integer
resource additions, project dependencies and mutually exclusive alternatives.
Demonstrate monitor procurement versus repairing and commissioning existing idle
equipment. Other required resources still limit usable capacity. The data model
can represent bundles affecting several integer resources; clinical standards,
staff scheduling, service reopening, construction, continuous supplies and
multi-service resource competition are outside this first implementation.

These are modeled funding proposals. They do not purchase equipment, approve a
budget, execute transfers or prove health outcomes. Recording the chosen action
and following its execution belongs to Feature 8.

## User workflow

1. Open a saved Feature 6 proposal and choose “Plan investments.”
2. Review the post-transfer baseline, inherited demand assumptions and unresolved
   bottlenecks. Show transfers as assumed for this comparison, not executed.
3. Enter an investment budget, planning start, evaluation date and operating-cost
   allowance period. Review/edit the small supplied project catalog.
4. Calculate the best package under the explicit objective and constraints.
5. Compare no additional investment with the proposed package. Show dependencies,
   operating costs, ready dates, resource changes, remaining gaps and exclusions.
6. Save/reopen the result. Changing budget or projects creates a new run.

## Shared agreement before splitting

Backend/data owns canonical Pydantic schemas, migrations and wire examples.
Optimization/data reviews every numerical field and owns the pure engine.
Frontend uses the same examples and generated types. All fields use snake_case,
existing IDs, UTC timestamps and USD integer cents. Do not calculate independent
capacity or project costs in the frontend.

Proposed endpoints in the existing investments module:

```text
GET  /api/v1/investments/catalog
POST /api/v1/investments/runs
GET  /api/v1/investments/runs/{investment_run_id}
```

Catalog GET returns a versioned synthetic starter catalog. The request carries
the complete reviewed candidate list; edits remain run-local and do not require
catalog CRUD in this milestone. Never silently reload a changed catalog for a
saved run.

Canonical `InvestmentRunRequest`:

| Field | Agreement |
| --- | --- |
| `allocation_run_id` | Saved validated allocation proposal with a proposed summary |
| `planning_starts_at` | Explicit UTC assumption that source transfers are completed |
| `evaluation_at` | UTC timestamp at which investment capacity is compared |
| `operating_cost_months` | Positive integer full-month allowance; explicit, no inferred prorating |
| `budget_minor_units` | Nonnegative investment budget, separate from already modeled transfer costs |
| `currency` | Literal `USD` for this milestone |
| `catalog_version` | Starter catalog version or explicit user-edited version label |
| `candidates` | Complete reviewed list of `InvestmentCandidate` objects |
| `assumptions` | Visible planning assumptions, including demand at evaluation time |

Demand and facility coverage initially inherit the saved allocation input. Demand
is assumed to persist to the evaluation date; do not turn daily forecast activity
into simultaneous demand automatically. Planning start must not precede incident
start plus the source allocation horizon. This is an explicit scenario assumption,
not evidence that the source proposal happened. Projects are assumed to proceed
in parallel, except for dependencies; workforce/project scheduling is not modeled.

Each `InvestmentCandidate` has:

- `candidate_id`, `name`, `facility_id`, `service_id`.
- `capital_cost_minor_units`, `monthly_operating_cost_minor_units`.
- `implementation_days`: nonnegative integer calendar days after dependencies are
  ready; costs and times are supplied estimates, never generated by the optimizer.
- `resource_effects`: list of `{resource_id, quantity_delta, unit}`. Deltas are
  nonnegative whole quantities for integer resources in the frozen service profile.
  A dependency-only project may have an empty list; duplicate effects are rejected.
- `requires_candidate_ids`: prerequisite IDs in this same catalog. Unknown IDs,
  self-dependencies and cycles are invalid.
- `exclusive_group_id`: nullable group; at most one candidate per group is selected.
- `source_note`, `confirmed_at`: human-reviewed provenance, including compatibility,
  space, staffing and recurring-cost assumptions. Missing required confirmation
  makes a candidate ineligible rather than supplying invented feasibility.

Asset repair alternatives share an exclusive group to prevent counting the same
restored equipment twice. Represent enabling work as dependency-only projects;
the final commissioned project owns the resource effect. Existing zero quantities
are not evidence that repairable assets exist; their existence and compatibility
must be explicitly supplied and confirmed in the project evidence.

Canonical `InvestmentRun` includes:

- Run/source IDs (`investment_run_id`, `allocation_run_id`, `simulation_run_id`,
  `incident_id`, `revision_id`, `baseline_id`), `created_at`, frozen `input`,
  engine/solver/schema versions and actual solver settings.
- `solver_status`: `not_run`, `optimal`, `feasible`, `infeasible`, `unknown`.
- `plan_outcome`: `needs_inputs`, `improved`, `no_improvement`, `no_solution`.
- `coverage`: inherited facility exclusions and candidate exclusions with reasons.
- `baseline_summary`, nullable `proposed_summary`: facility rows with capacity,
  required/covered/unmet service places and bottlenecks; scoped network totals.
- `selected_projects`: candidate IDs, ready timestamps, resource effects, capital,
  operating allowance and total costs. Include prerequisite-only projects.
- `total_capital_cost_minor_units`, `total_operating_cost_minor_units`,
  `total_cost_minor_units`, `remaining_budget_minor_units`.
- `remaining_gap_reasons`, `validation_passed`, `review_required`,
  `solve_duration_ms`, objective description and visible assumptions.

Errors follow the existing shared envelope: field-level 422 for invalid requests,
404 for missing source/run. A source with no validated proposed summary is not
eligible for investment planning; explain this in a 422 source-field error.
Unknown/stale source facilities remain excluded. Missing candidate evidence yields
explicit candidate exclusions. No usable source coverage yields `needs_inputs`.
An empty or entirely ineligible catalog can yield `no_improvement` with reasons.
Internal solver/model/validation failures return a server error, never a saved
apparently valid funding proposal.

Before integrating, publish matching examples in `contracts/examples/feature-7/`
for improved, no improvement, incomplete inputs and timeout without a plan.
Regenerate OpenAPI and frontend API types when actual schemas are committed.

## Engine agreement and constraints

Canonical pure interface:

```python
optimize_investments(input: InvestmentEngineInput) -> InvestmentEngineResult
```

Use shared types. Input contains post-transfer effective snapshots, frozen
requirement profiles, inherited demand/coverage, reviewed candidates and planning
constraints. Build it through public allocation/scenario services; do not read
another module's repository. Reapply saved transfers and verify the resulting
capacity matches the allocation's saved proposed summary. Do not query live
observations or apply transfers twice.

Numerical rules:

1. Select each candidate at most once using OR-Tools CP-SAT. Selecting a project
   requires every prerequisite, including its cost. Enforce exclusive groups.
2. Compute earliest readiness from the acyclic dependency graph:
   `ready_at = max(planning_starts_at, prerequisite ready dates) + implementation_days`.
   Only projects ready by `evaluation_at` are eligible; exclude later projects and
   their dependents with reasons. No partial benefits before commissioning.
3. Per-project cost is capital plus monthly operating cost times
   `operating_cost_months`. Charge the full allowance for each selected project,
   irrespective of ready date; this is an explicit conservative budgeting rule.
   It is not a discounted cash-flow or lifetime-cost estimate. Total cannot exceed
   budget. Do not charge prior transfer costs again.
4. Apply additive resource effects once to frozen post-transfer quantities. Only
   operating, included services with known required evidence can gain capacity.
   Buying equipment cannot silently reopen unavailable/limited services.
5. Recalculate capacity using all existing profile requirements. Demand remains a
   soft target; covered demand is the lesser of capacity and required places.
   Do not credit benefits beyond demand or add incompatible service units.
6. Primary objective: minimize unmet modeled demand across included facilities at
   the evaluation date. Among proven primary optima, minimize total cost, then
   project count. Use sequential solves under one server-side 5-second total limit;
   freeze an objective only when proven optimal. Expose feasible/unknown honestly.
7. Independently verify costs, dependencies, timing, exclusivity, resource effects
   and capacities before persistence. Selecting no projects is allowed. An optimal
   result can leave a gap; lack of improvement is not proven infeasibility.
8. Save all inputs, evidence, selected candidates and outputs. Reloading returns
   that result; changes to catalogs/observations/source proposals never rewrite it.

No new OpenAI integration is needed for the first release. Human-reviewed catalog
inputs plus deterministic constraint explanations are sufficient. A later AI
proposal reviewer can identify missing evidence or explain the computed package,
but it must not invent prices, dependencies, available assets or numerical benefits.

## Shared demonstration

Continue the Feature 6 fixture: A has 3 monitors and demand 3; B has 3 monitors,
18 beds, 9 nurses and demand 8. The five-place gap at B is monitor-limited.
Budget: 300000 USD cents ($3,000); operating allowance: 3 months; evaluation:
30 days after the explicitly assumed transfer completion.

| Candidate | Capital | Monthly operation | Time | Effect/dependency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buy five compatible monitors | $5,000 | $300 | 14 days | +5 working monitors |
| Buy one compatible monitor | $1,000 | $60 | 14 days | +1 working monitor |
| Repair five confirmed idle monitors | $1,000 | $0 | 7 days | No working-resource effect until commissioned |
| Commission repaired monitors | $500 | $300 | 3 days after repair | Requires repair; +5 working monitors |

All effects/costs/assets are fictional, explicitly reviewed scenario inputs.
Expected selection: repair + commissioning, ready after 10 days, total cost
$1,500 capital + $900 operating allowance = $2,400. B capacity increases 3->8;
covered network demand increases 6->11 and unmet demand decreases 5->0. Buying
five is over budget; buying one costs $1,180 and closes only one place. The repair
alone cannot add working monitors. Lower the budget or move evaluation before
commissioning to demonstrate a changed recommendation/no improvement.

Build order: shared schemas/examples and catalog -> engine/API/UI independently
against those contracts -> connect this one scenario -> backend/numerical checks
and user web review. Feature 8 then records which proposal was chosen and tracks
execution and subsequently reported outcomes.
